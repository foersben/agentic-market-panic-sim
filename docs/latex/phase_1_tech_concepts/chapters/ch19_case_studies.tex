\section{Case Study 1: The Liquidity Black Hole (Lehman Scenario)}

The most severe shock modeled in AMPS mimics the systemic failure of a foundational financial institution. The God Agent broadcasts an 8-K filing indicating the instantaneous insolvency of a Tier-1 Prime Broker. 

The intended microstructural outcome is a \textit{Liquidity Black Hole}. The Algorithmic Market Makers (AMMs) must mathematically assume that $100\%$ of incoming order flow is toxic (informed). According to the Glosten-Milgrom formulation (Chapter 3), they must instantly widen their spreads to infinity or withdraw entirely.

\section{Case Study 2: The Flash Rally (Short Squeeze)}

Conversely, panic can manifest as an upward violent explosion in price, typically driven by a "Short Squeeze." In this scenario, the God Agent broadcasts news of an unexpected regulatory approval or a massive corporate acquisition. 

Agents who are heavily short the asset (borrowed shares sold in anticipation of a price drop) are forced to buy the asset back instantly to cover their positions, regardless of the price. This aggressive market-buying consumes all Ask liquidity, driving the price up vertically.

\section{Case Study 3: The Fat-Finger Anomaly}

The final case study models a purely mechanical, non-informational panic. The Macro God Agent is silent. Instead, a single institutional micro-agent suffers a simulated execution error (a "Fat Finger"), accidentally appending two extra zeros to a market sell order, dumping 1,000,000 shares instead of 10,000.

Because this is not a macroeconomic shock, there is no true information asymmetry. The AMMs initially widen their spreads due to the massive Order Flow Imbalance (OFI), but quickly recognize the lack of corresponding macro news and step back in to provide stabilizing liquidity, "buying the dip."
