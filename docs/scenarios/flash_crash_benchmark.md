# The Flash Crash Benchmark

Traditional Agent-Based Models rely on rigid heuristics that fail to capture the bounded rationality and emotional contagion of real human populations during crisis events. The systemic collapse of a financial market during a flash crash is rarely driven by rational equilibrium equations. Instead, it is an emergent phenomenon driven by messy, profoundly human factors: a rumor propagating through a retail trading forum, the sudden emotional panic of over-leveraged day traders, and the relentless, cascading liquidations triggered by institutional algorithms.

To accurately model this, Phase 1 of the AMPS project establishes the **Flash Crash Benchmark**. This scenario isolates and measures exactly how much irrational, generative herd behavior is required to overwhelm a strictly algorithmic liquidity pool.

## 1. The Stable Baseline (Algorithmic Market Makers)

Before any chaos is introduced, the engine must establish a provably stable financial equilibrium.

The baseline of the simulation is driven entirely by deterministic algorithms written in Numba. These **Inventory-Aware Market Makers** continuously post passive bids and asks to the Limit Order Book (LOB). They operate on strict mathematical rules:

* If inventory runs too long, they skew prices lower.
* If inventory runs too short, they skew prices higher.
* Under normal conditions, they provide infinite, stable liquidity to the market.

## 2. The Disruptors (The Generative Swarm)

Into this algorithmic equilibrium, we inject the LLM swarm. These generative agents act as disruptive, panic-susceptible market participants. To simulate a real market, they are divided into heterogeneous personas:

* **Retail Panic Traders:** Highly susceptible to narrative shocks and emotional contagion.
* **Institutional Hedgers:** Slower to react, but capable of executing massive, systemic block liquidations.
* **Momentum Chasers:** Programmed to ignore fundamental news and strictly follow the velocity of the LOB.

## 3. The Central Orchestrator & The Macro Shock

The simulation operates in discrete time steps (ticks). At a predetermined tick, the **70B God Agent** (running on the CPU) acts as the central orchestrator and generates a "Breaking News" macroeconomic shock (e.g., a catastrophic supply chain failure or a geopolitical crisis).

This narrative shock is broadcast asynchronously to the generative swarm.

## 4. The Panic Cascade

Upon receiving the shock, the LLM agents execute their cognitive loops:

1. **Observation:** They read the breaking news and inspect the depth of the current Limit Order Book.
2. **Reasoning:** They generate a nuanced, internal reasoning trace (e.g., assessing the risk of holding their current inventory).
3. **Semantic Intent:** They output a constrained JSON schema expressing their trading intent (e.g., `SELL 100% of inventory at TAKER prices`).

Because hundreds of LLM agents are reasoning independently but reacting to the same systemic shock, their simultaneous actions flood the Numba event bus.

## 5. The Objective Measurement

The primary benchmark is to measure the exact tipping point of the flash crash.

As the generative agents panic sell simultaneously, they consume all the bids provided by the Algorithmic Market Makers. As the Market Makers accumulate massive long inventories, their deterministic algorithms force them to drop their bid prices exponentially to avoid bankruptcy.

This creates a **liquidity spiral**. The scenario is complete when we can map the correlation between the severity of the narrative shock generated by the 70B God Agent and the depth of the resulting flash crash in the Numba Limit Order Book.
